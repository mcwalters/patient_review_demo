What the data actually is

Four findings that changed the design

I audited the data before building anything. Each of these closed off an obvious product and opened a better one.

Finding Evidence What it changed
The notes are generated from the tables 153 of 153 fields agree — conditions, medications, BP, BMI No extraction — it would be a lossy reimplementation of a join. The notes became a check on the brief instead, and that check finds three encounters where the note says labs were ordered and not one came back.
The two lab tables do not join 0 of 1,288 ORDER_PROC_ID match; values and dates disagree Kept separate, each keyed to the encounter. An invented linkage would have been 60% fabricated rows.
No medication is ever stopped END_DATE and DISCON_TIME 100% null; all 522 read Active “Patient is on X” is unanswerable. “Patient never had X” still holds — which is why care gaps survive as findings and duplicate‑therapy claims do not.
Systolic and diastolic generated independently 27 of 100 have an impossible pulse pressure; one reads 108/111 Blood pressure is staged against ACC/AHA thresholds, and implausible readings are separated out as data defects rather than clinical findings.

How I found the third one

The review reported “clinically dangerous triple anticoagulation” — one patient on three DOACs at once. Alarming, specific, repeated in three runs. The start dates are March 2023, July 2024 and December 2025: sequential switching, read as concurrent because nothing is ever recorded as stopped. The data defect and the wrong claim are the same fact.

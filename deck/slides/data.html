Finding Evidence What the product does about it
The notes are generated from the tables Across 153 notes, zero diagnoses and zero medications appear that are not already rows. Strip the conditions, drugs, BP and BMI and what is left is four fixed sentences — one per template, no variation. No extraction: there is no clinical fact in the prose to extract. But those four sentences assert that care was delivered — “labs ordered per guideline intervals” — and no column records that. So the notes became a check on the brief, and it finds three encounters where the note claims labs were ordered and not one ever resulted.
The Clarity foreign key is broken order_results.ORDER_PROC_ID is populated on all 1,288 rows, well‑formed, and resolves to none of the 588 orders — across the same 153 encounters, so nothing is merely out of scope. Falling back to patient‑plus‑analyte falsely resolves 85 of 120 pending orders. Kept separate, each keyed to the encounter. “Never resulted” is read from a single row, never from a join — the only reason it is a finding rather than a fabrication. Note this is a defect in this extract: in a real Clarity warehouse that key is the documented path and you would use it.
No medication is ever stopped END_DATE and DISCON_TIME 100% null; all 522 read Active “Patient is on X” is unanswerable. “Patient never had X” still holds — which is why care gaps survive as findings and duplicate‑therapy claims do not.
Systolic and diastolic generated independently 27 of 100 have an impossible pulse pressure; one reads 108/111 Blood pressure is staged against ACC/AHA thresholds, and implausible readings are separated out as data defects rather than clinical findings.

ICD‑10 codes fragment across near‑duplicates, so cohorts are built on the 3‑character category. Hypertension is 46 patients across two codes, not 25 — grouping on the raw code halves it.

Provider attribution is random (χ²/df = 0.94), so nothing in the product infers anything from who authored a note. Some authors are credentialled DVM.

This is the slide to spend time on. The notes finding is the one they are most likely to push on, because reasoning over unstructured notes was their own example. Lead with the audit, not the decision. The strong form is not "the fields agree" - it is that across 153 notes not one diagnosis and not one drug appears that is not already a row, and that masking the structured values leaves exactly four fixed sentences with no variation at all. There is no patient-specific prose in this extract: no symptom, no plan, no sentence a clinician composed. Then the turn: those four sentences do carry something no column does, a claim that care happened, which is unverifiable from the tables and is why checking beats extracting here. Two commands reproduce both halves. Then say where the model went instead. Row three is the one that shapes the most: because nothing is ever recorded as stopped, the product can say a patient never had a drug and cannot say a patient is on one, so care gaps are findings and duplicate-therapy claims are not. Row two is the one to dwell on if they ask whether I understood the data, and it is the one to state carefully. These are Epic Clarity table and column names, and in Clarity ORDER_RESULTS is a child of ORDER_PROC joined on ORDER_PROC_ID - so the join is not something I invented, it is the documented relationship. This extract declares that key, populates it on all 1,288 rows with well-formed ids in the same range, and resolves none of them, across the same 153 encounters in both tables. It is a broken foreign key, not a scoping gap, and I checked the innocent explanation before concluding that. The fallback join a reasonable person then reaches for returns rows - just the wrong ones. Seven of every ten pending orders would have picked up a result belonging to a different order, and the follow-up capability would have vanished into a smaller, confident, wrong number. That is why 74 real open orders is a number I can stand behind. Two findings held back for questions rather than crowding the slide. ICD-10 codes fragment across near-duplicates, so grouping on the raw code halves most cohorts - hypertension is 46 patients across two codes, not 25; it is the single best example of real preprocessing, silent and quantified.

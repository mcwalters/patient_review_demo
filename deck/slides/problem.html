The problem

Attention is the scarce resource, not information

A population‑health nurse working a panel between visits can read a chart quickly — trained reviewers clear six to twenty a day. Ninety‑four of these hundred patients have at least one abnormal lab. Reading was never the bottleneck. What each finding costs after you read it is — the call, the order, the chase — and so is deciding which ones earn it.

What I built is the weekly worklist. Agents work three questions — which records cannot be trusted, who is missing recommended therapy, what was started and never finished — and every patient they name opens a pre‑visit brief.

A supervisor briefs three specialists and consults them at once. It never chose whether to call each — the answer was always all three — and sequencing them bought nothing, because no specialist can read another’s findings. Running them concurrently took a review from 311s to 159s. The split itself is still unproven: A/B‑tested against one agent holding the same tools, it has yet to earn its complexity.

WHERE "six to twenty a day" COMES FROM, if asked: the 2025 CDI Week Industry Survey, reported in the ACDIS Q&A on Productivity. Most respondents complete 6-10 new patient reviews per day (59.78%), then 11-15 (18.95%); the CDI specialist interviewed runs an expected daily average of 20 including re-reviews. Give the caveat before they do - that is inpatient clinical documentation integrity, a different role and setting from population-health panel work, which carries outreach the CDI number does not. It is cited as evidence that READING is fast, which is the point, not as a throughput target for this user. An earlier version of this slide claimed a nurse reviews about fifteen a week; that was an assumption I could not source, and the argument is stronger without it. The third number is the one to dwell on - it is why data judgment came before any modelling. The concurrency line and the A/B line are two different claims - the first is settled, the second is not. Concurrent consulting is measured and merged: 311s to 159s over three runs, ranges that do not overlap, same findings, floor intact. Whether three specialists beat one agent is still open. Do not over-claim the A/B: n=3 an arm, the single-agent instruction has had far less tuning, and the canary gap shrinks to 4-of-8 versus 3-of-3 once my own five-run eval is pooled in. The latency gap is the robust part - 267 to 405 seconds against 98 to 162, no overlap. If they push, the answer is that I would ship the single agent and keep the floor, the structured store and the uncited-high check, because the A/B held those constant and neither arm drives uncited findings to zero.
